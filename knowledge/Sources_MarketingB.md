## **Document 1 — Retention & Lifetime Value Brief**

**GreenThreads Marketing — Retention & Lifetime Value Brief** **Prepared for:** Denver launch planning · **Date:** July 2026

**Objective.** Understand which customers are worth the most over time, not just the cheapest to acquire.

**Headline finding.** Customers we acquire through **Email** are our most valuable. Email-acquired customers show an average lifetime value of **$403** across **3.8 orders** each. By contrast, **Instagram-acquired** customers — the channel our paid-social team favors — show an average lifetime value of just **$134** across 1.6 orders, the lowest of any channel.

**Recommendation.** Invest in email capture and a loyalty program at the Denver launch. If we can lift the repeat-purchase rate of new customers to 60%, retention could become our biggest growth lever.

**Supporting note.** LTV figures are drawn from our existing customer base by acquisition channel. The 60% repeat-rate target is a planning goal, not a current result.

## **Document 2 — Customer Feedback Summary**

**GreenThreads — Customer Review & Feedback Summary (Austin store \+ online)**

A summary of recent customer feedback, grouped by theme. Verbatims are representative, lightly edited for length.

**What repeat customers say they value.**

* “The quality holds up. I’ve washed the joggers a hundred times and they still look new — that’s why I keep coming back.”

* “I trust the sustainability. It’s the reason I buy here instead of the big brands.”

* “Fit is consistent across styles. Once I found my size I just reorder.”

**What first-time buyers mention.**

* “Loved the store experience, staff really knew the products.”

* “A little pricier than I expected, but I understand what I’m paying for.”

* “Wish there were more color options in the bottoms.”

**Signals.**

* Repeat purchase is driven mostly by **product quality, sustainability trust, and fit consistency** — not by discounts or promotions.

* Price is noticed by first-time buyers but rarely cited by repeat buyers.

* No customer in this sample mentioned a loyalty or points program as a reason they return.

## **Document 3 — Loyalty Program Proposal (Draft)**

**GreenThreads Marketing — Denver Loyalty Program Proposal (draft)**

**Proposal.** Launch a points-based loyalty program alongside the Denver opening. Customers earn points per dollar, redeemable for discounts.

**Projected impact.** We project the program could lift the new-customer repeat-purchase rate to **60%** within the first year, materially increasing lifetime value.

**Estimated cost.** Program platform and rewards liability estimated at a modest percentage of loyalty-member revenue; setup handled by existing CRM tools, no new headcount.

**Assumptions.**

* The 60% repeat rate is a **target**, based on category benchmarks, not on GreenThreads’ own results to date.

* The proposal assumes points and discounts are what drive repeat purchase.

**Open question flagged by the team.** Our own customer feedback suggests repeat purchase is driven by quality, sustainability, and fit — not discounts. Before we commit, we should confirm a points program is the right lever, or whether the same investment in product experience and email would do more.
